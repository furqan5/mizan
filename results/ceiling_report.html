LSI is a calcium-carbonate index. It is computed from pH, calcium and alkalinity, and it carries no information about sulfate, silica or phosphate. Where the binding mineral is not calcite, LSI cannot rank which species deposits first — it can read comfortable while another mineral is already supersaturated.

The most you could ever save is 33.3 %

At 3.0 cycles your blowdown is 33.3 % of your makeup, and evaporation is the rest. Evaporation is the heat rejection and cannot be recovered, so no measure of any kind — control, treatment, recycle — can save more than that. Reaching 20 % would need 25 % RO recovery on the blowdown.

What a controller may cost and still pay

We will not quote you a payback, because a payback needs an installed cost and we do not have one — nor, as far as we can find, does anyone: a search of the Internet Archive's PDF collection across fifteen cost, controls and retrofit queries returned nothing citable on what a cooling-tower supervisory controller costs installed. So the question is inverted. At $88,976/year on this duty, the most the system may cost installed and still pay back is $177,952 over two years, $266,928 over three, and $444,881 over five. These are bounds, not prices. They also assume the site actually reaches the ceiling above, which is a SCALING ceiling and therefore an upper bound in its own right — the operating ceiling is the minimum over several constraints this model does not compute.

Phosphate is measured at 1.0 mg/L. Calcium phosphate is kinetically inhibited — it tolerates far more supersaturation than carbonate before it deposits — so what matters is not the index alone but which inhibitor programme is dosed.

A costed engineering study of a precipitation-softening train (DiFilippo, Sylvan Source, Oct 2023 - 1 MGD, $25.4 M installed, $2.79 M/yr in chemicals) puts the real cost at $2.02/m³ in chemicals alone and $2.94/m³ with capital over twenty years. Side-stream treatment pays where discharge is prohibited and the alternative is zero liquid discharge - a regulatory driver, not a water-price one.
