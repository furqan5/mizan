## 2 — The problem, in one number

A cooling tower evaporates water. The minerals left behind concentrate. **Cycles of concentration** is how far you push that before they deposit on the heat exchanger.

| | |
|---|---|
| What operators actually run on recycled water | **3.0 cycles** |
| What the chemistry on that same water allows | **4.52 cycles** |
| Water that gap is worth | **14.4 %** of makeup |

**Five independent operators, two continents, all between 2 and 4 cycles** — a Saudi refinery, a Qatari district-cooling utility, a US federal pilot, a food plant, a university campus. None of them is badly run. They are running on caution, because nothing tells them where the limit is.

## 3 — Why nobody knows where the limit is

The industry sets its water limit with the **Langelier Saturation Index** — published **1936**, and it sees exactly one mineral: calcium carbonate.

On the recycled water that regulation is now forcing plants onto, calcium carbonate is **not** what binds.

| Mineral | Solubility vs temperature | Binds at |
|---|---|---|
| Calcite, calcium phosphate, brucite | Retrograde — less soluble hot | the **hot condenser skin** |
| **Amorphous silica** | **Prograde — less soluble cold** | the **cold basin** |

Two consequences the incumbent index cannot express:

1. **A single-temperature index is structurally wrong.** Different minerals bind at opposite ends of the same loop.
2. **Acid cannot buy cycles against silica.** Below pH 9 dissolved silica is neutral H₄SiO₄, so its saturation is pH-independent. Acid works on carbonate and does nothing here — which is why acid-based programmes hit a wall on recycled water.

## 4 — What Mizan computes instead

Full aqueous speciation — ion association, Davies activity coefficients, free vs total ions — **benchmarked against PHREEQC 3.9.0.**

Saturation indices for calcite, gypsum, amorphous silica, tricalcium phosphate, hydroxyapatite and brucite, each evaluated **where that mineral is least soluble**, not at one convenient temperature.

Then a supervisory controller co-optimises **fan speed, blowdown rate and acid dose** against those limits — instead of holding a conductivity setpoint somebody guessed.

## 6 — The data-centre case

24-hour coupled study. Blind free cooling vs chemically-bounded, 5 cycles, silica floor **31.8 °C**.

| Region | IT load | PUE b→c | WUE b→c | Peak silica saturation b→c | Scaling hours b→c |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dhahran | 9 MW | 3.537 → 3.556 | 1.96 → 1.94 | 1.028 → **1.000** | 12 → **0** |
| Loudoun County | 20 MW | 1.049 → 1.159 | 1.88 → 1.74 | 1.178 → **1.000** | 24 → **0** |
| Frankfurt | 20 MW | 1.041 → 1.350 | 1.58 → 1.57 | 1.314 → 1.121 | 20 → 8 |
| Balloki | 8 MW | 1.083 → 1.162 | 2.07 → 1.92 | 1.165 → **1.000** | 21 → **0** |

**Blind free cooling runs the loop supersaturated in silica for 12 to 24 hours out of every 24.** It is depositing on the exchanger, and nothing an operator watches — PUE, WUE, approach — shows it happening.

> **Do not sell this as an energy saving. It is an energy cost.** Bounded control costs +$107k/yr in Dhahran, +$1.6M/yr in Loudoun, +$9.8M/yr in Frankfurt. What it buys is knowing where the floor is. Say that plainly; a data-centre operator will check.

**Frankfurt's 8 remaining hours are not a control failure.** They are hours where the tower over-cools at its slowest usable fan and the floor is physically unreachable. Those are the *same eight hours* the controller flags — so the site needs a **tower bypass**. That's a valve you specify before you build, not a caveat you explain afterwards.

## 8 — Product

A retrofit **edge controller**: sensor skid + motorised blowdown valve + edge compute, speaking BACnet/Modbus. Capex plus an annual model-recalibration licence.

Ships **read-only in shadow mode**. Blowdown and dosing stay advisory until site validation. Deterministic fixed-step solver, bounded runtime, documented fallback to incumbent setpoints on solver failure or sensor loss.

**The skid deliberately does not measure the chemistry, and that is the central design decision.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Online silica + calcium + alkalinity + phosphate | **$120,000–185,000 per tower** |
| Annual saving on a 4.2 MW tower | **$89,000** |

The instruments cost more than the thing they optimise. That is structural, and it is why no incumbent sells chemistry-bounded control.

So the skid buys only what's cheap — toroidal conductivity, pH, ORP, Pt100, two magmeters, a coupon rack: **~$15k in instruments, $23–30k installed**, against a computed break-even bound of **$267k** at three-year payback. The chemistry is *computed*, and the conductivity sensor that's already there checks the computation for free: specific conductance is a known function of ion composition, so when measured falls below predicted, ions have left solution. That's the scaling alarm, for nothing.

> Toroidal not contacting, for a specific reason: a fouled contacting cell **reads low**, so a bleed controller stops blowing down. The sensor failure drives the system deeper into scaling.

## 9 — Why the tagline is a spec, not a slogan

| Claim | Module | What it says on the benchmark water |
|---|---|---|
| **chemistry-aware** | ion association + Davies, vs PHREEQC 3.9.0 | ceiling 4.52 cycles, bound by calcite |
| **metallurgy-safe** | Larson–Skold + chloride pitting | 316 stainless caps at **1.85 cycles** |
| **permit-compliant** | RCER-2015 discharge tables | permit binds at **3.33 cycles** on nitrate |

A result worth stating because it inverts the usual objection: **raising cycles moves Larson–Skold by exactly zero** — it's a ratio of anions, and concentrating multiplies every ion equally. Acid moves it from **4.60 to 55.0**. So the corrosion exposure rides entirely on the acid lever, and that lever is separately switchable. A Jubail site under RCER may not dose acid at all, and therefore takes none of this risk.
