# One-minute founder video — notes beside the lens

**Not a script.** YZi say a recital flattens exactly what they're listening for. These are beats and anchor phrases. Say it the way you'd say it to a friend.

Solo founder, so it's just you in frame. Phone camera, daylight, quiet room, no edit.

**Sixty seconds is about 150 spoken words.** That is far less than it feels. The four beats below are already at the limit — if you add a sentence, cut a sentence.

---

## The four beats

### 1 · Who — 10 seconds

> Furqan, energy engineer, Lahore. Building Mizan. Solo, six weeks in, full time.

Say "solo" out loud. Don't hide it — they read the application, and a founder who says it plainly reads as someone who knows what they're missing.

### 2 · What — 20 seconds

> Cooling towers evaporate water and the minerals left behind concentrate. Every tower has a ceiling on how far you can push that. The whole industry uses an index that only sees calcium carbonate — but on recycled water the thing that actually stops you is silica, and nobody's index can see it.
>
> So plants run at three cycles on caution. On the water I've modelled, the real limit is four and a half.

**The gap between 3 and 4.5 is the entire company.** If only one sentence survives, it's that one.

### 3 · Why this one — 20 seconds

> A technician who opens condensers for a district cooling utility told me the deposit he scrapes out is dark brown and black, with white between it. He calls it lime scale. Lime scale is white.
>
> He's been cleaning that equipment for years and nobody has ever told him what's on it. That's the problem I couldn't leave alone.

This is your strongest thirty seconds in the whole application. It's concrete, it's someone else's voice, and it's the moment you learned your own model was incomplete. Don't rush it, and don't explain it afterwards — let it land.

### 4 · Where it's going — 10 seconds

> It's one controller for two markets now — Saudi district cooling and liquid-cooled data centres, same physics underneath.
>
> Fifty defects logged against my own model. Two gates still failing, both published. What I want next is one real tower, metered.

Ending on what you still need beats ending on a claim. It's also true.

## Delivery

| | |
|---|---|
| **Look at the lens**, not the screen | Notes go *beside* the camera, not below it |
| **One take is fine** | Stumbles are explicitly allowed. A clean recital is worse |
| **Don't say "we"** | You're solo. "We" invites a question you don't want asked on camera |
| **No demo, no slides, no music** | They say this twice. There's a separate field for the product |
| **Say a number, not an adjective** | "Three cycles, real limit four and a half" — not "significant savings" |

---

## Three things NOT to say

- **"Runs Saudi district cooling plants."** It runs none. Say "built to run" or "I've modelled." The verb is the one thing a technical reviewer will check.
- **"Saves 15% of water."** Your own annual study says the water saving is *negative* inside the envelope you've validated. Don't put a number on camera that your repo contradicts.
- **"AI-powered."** You have a first-principles physics engine. Saying AI invites the assumption that you fitted a black box to data, which is the opposite of what makes this defensible.

---

## If you get a second take

Swap beat 3 for the other true story: your fix for a defect reproduced the same defect, you noticed the pattern had happened six times, and you wrote a test that maps every constraint to the file that must enforce it — it caught an eighth instance on its first run.

Beat 3 as written is more human. This one is more impressive to an engineer. Use the technician.
