# Staged defects from `feat/safety-interlocks`, for the register

**17 September 2026.** Staged, not merged into `docs/defect_register.md`: the register, its counts and `HANDOFF.md` are edited by whoever integrates the branches. Numbers 54, 55 and 56 are reserved for this branch. Every figure below was produced by code on this branch.

| # | Defect | How it showed up | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 54 | **The saturation state the optimiser enforces leaves out the sulfate its own acid dose adds** | `evaluate_operating_point` and `_cost_at_ph` evaluate every index on `makeup_water.concentrate(cycles)`, so the circulating water carries makeup sulfate × C and nothing from the acid. Sulfuric acid leaves one mole of sulfate per mole dosed. `corrosion.acid_driven_index` already models this for Larson–Skold, and UFC 3-230-13 §5-2.1.2.2 (quoted in `corrosion.py`) names the mechanism, but the gypsum index never got it. On the field-validated makeup, with the dose from `acid_dose_for_ph` at 30 °C, the acid adds **386.5 mg/L at 5 cycles, pH 8.0**, and **469.1 at 6 cycles**. That puts `SI_gypsum` too low by **0.0815** (30 °C) and **0.0830** (45 °C) at 5 cycles, and by 0.0825 / 0.0841 at 6. The error is non-conservative: it runs in the direction that scales a condenser. The same omission hides sulfate from any concrete check (`src/concrete.py`): with acid, ACI S2 is reached at **3.99** cycles against 5.00 without. The alkalinity is not reduced either, which makes calcite *over*stated, the conservative direction | **Open.** Not fixed on this branch, because changing `controller.py`'s optimisation behaviour is out of scope here. On this water, gypsum with the acid sulfate included is still below zero at 6 cycles (−0.1549 at 30 °C), so the omission does not reach a binding limit here. **Nobody has checked whether any published headline number moves.** On a high-sulfate makeup, where gypsum is the wall, it would |
| 55 | **The engine's pH closure has no lower validity limit and returns a negative pH once alkalinity runs out** | `chemistry.ph_atmospheric_equilibrium` computes `[H+] = K1·KH·pCO2 / [HCO3-]` with bicarbonate clamped at 1e-12, so at zero alkalinity it returns **pH −0.75**. That formula is correct only while bicarbonate carries the alkalinity. It cannot represent excess strong acid, which is exactly the regime an acid-overdose incident enters. Nothing warns the caller. Any safety simulation built on it would report nonsense past the equivalence point | **Guarded, not fixed.** `chemistry.py` internals belong to another branch. `src/safety_sim.py` uses the proton balance on the engine's own constants instead. It matches the engine where bicarbonate dominates, gives CO2-saturated water at zero alkalinity (between pH 5.4 and 5.8) and pH 3.00 at −1 meq/kg. `test_the_engines_closure_cannot_represent_excess_acid` pins both. Suggested fix: a validity guard, or the same proton balance, inside `chemistry.py` |
| 56 | **The specified fail-safe, blowdown to 3.0 cycles on any trip, drains the basin faster during the one incident the interlocks exist for** | Moving the cycles setpoint to 3.0 is right for a chemistry trip and wrong when makeup is lost. A conductivity bleed sees the loop above its new setpoint and opens, discarding water that cannot be replaced. In the pre-registered incident, the basin reached the low-level pump trip at **90.8 min** with interlocks, against **101.1 min** with none. The protection layer shortened the time to losing circulation. It was specified that way before anyone ran it; the simulation found it | **Mitigated, default unchanged.** `InterlockConfig.hold_blowdown_on_makeup_loss` (not pre-registered, **off by default** so the matrix tests what was registered) holds the bleed closed while makeup is unproven. Low level then comes at **158.6 min**. Held by `test_registered_failsafe_empties_the_basin_sooner_on_makeup_loss` and `test_holding_blowdown_on_makeup_loss_keeps_the_inventory_longer`. Whether it should become the default is an engineering decision for whoever owns the specification |

## Failed gates, not defects

The register separates these, and so does this file. Two rows of the pre-registered fault matrix **fail as registered**. The matrix has not been changed.

| row | registered bound | measured | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| F3, pH probe drift | acid off within 61 s of the disagreement first exceeding 0.3 | **149 s** | the setpoint needs the disagreement held for 60 s, and ±0.005 pH noise on each probe resets the timer while a 0.5 pH/h drift passes through the band |
| F4a, fouled conductivity cell | trip within 601 s of SCI first exceeding 15 % | **768 s** | the same mechanism, on the residual |

Neither fault moved the true basin pH. The underlying inconsistency is between the bound and the setpoint as written in `docs/staged/safety_preregistration.md`: the bound counts from the first crossing, the setpoint from a continuous dwell. A fix, such as a leaky dwell integrator or a hysteresis band, would need its own pre-registration. It is not applied here.
